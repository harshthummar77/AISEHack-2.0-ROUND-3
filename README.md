There is **no plot-level ground-truth yield anywhere in this track**, so a supervised regressor
has nothing to fit. The model is a hybrid in which the radar sets the *ranking and spread* between
plots and official district statistics set the *level* — an explicit assumption, not a hidden one.

```
Y_p = Y_ref(c) · S(c) · exp( σ_c·ρ·z_p − ½(σ_c·ρ)² )
```

The lognormal form makes the area-weighted village mean reproduce the district anchor **by
construction**, so the model cannot invent a village-scale yield the radar cannot support.

## Pipeline

`pipeline/` scripts run in numeric order. `sarlib.py` (RPC model, calibration, geocoding) and
`cropmodel.py` (agronomic knowledge base) are the two libraries; everything else is a stage.

1. **Measurement** — Capella SLC ships as `beta_nought`, so β⁰ = (|DN|·scale)² and γ⁰ = β⁰·tan θ,
   with incidence computed per range sample from the product state vectors. Geocoding uses our own
   RPC00B implementation: **0.0000 px RMS against the 225 GCPs embedded in each product**.
   Terrain-referenced with Copernicus GLO-30; the ellipsoid/geoid datum is *solved* from those GCPs
   (N = −62.02 m, scene-to-scene sd 0.12 m). Residual inter-pass co-registration ≤ 0.12 m.
2. **Features** — every feature is a *village anomaly* (plot minus village median on the same date),
   which cancels gain, incidence response, look direction and common soil moisture in one step.
   Differences are taken only between the two geometry-safe pairs (Δθ 0.08° and 1.78°).
3. **Crop labels** — Round-1/2 carry-forward, area-constrained transportation LP.
4. **Yield forecast** — stage-weighted SAR index, district anchor, Monte Carlo with systematic and
   plot-independent errors separated.
5. **Aggregation** — area-weighted by crop, plus crop-mix scenarios.

Everything between the complex SLC and the output CSV is built from the Capella metadata and the
RPC model. **No SNAP, no ISCE, no `gdalwarp`.**

## Validation, without labels

Two Sentinel-2 scenes are *same-day coincident* with Capella passes. NDVI never enters the model —
which is the only thing that makes the test meaningful.

| Crop | ρ vs same-day NDVI (13 Oct) | p | Field status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rice | **+0.365** | 2×10⁻⁸ | standing |
| Cotton | **+0.361** | 1×10⁻¹² | standing |
| Groundnut | +0.112 | 0.079 | harvesting |
| Maize | +0.019 | 0.88 | harvested |
| Bajra | −0.071 | 0.61 | harvested |

Significant for the two crops still in the field, **statistically null for the three already cut**,
where both sensors see bare soil. An artefact would correlate everywhere or nowhere.

**Why SAR at all, measured:** of 46 Sentinel-2 overpasses June–November, 10 were usable. Between
19 June and 8 October there were 29 overpasses and **none below 23.3% cloud** — a 111-day blackout
across sowing, establishment and peak growth. All six SAR passes were unaffected.

## Things we tried and did not use

- **Repeat-pass InSAR.** Of 15 pass pairs, 5 are opposite-look and 9 exceed the critical baseline;
  the one viable pair (B⊥ 844 m vs B⊥crit 3975 m) is 56 days apart. Structurally unavailable.
- **Sub-aperture (sub-look) coherence.** Single-pass, so immune to temporal decorrelation. The
  estimator verifies correctly (point scatterers γ = 0.98 vs 0.25 for clutter) but is null at plot
  level — repeatability r = 0.02, crop η² = 0.02. Reported as a null.
- **Our own re-derived crop map.** It agreed with the carry-forward on only 46% of observed plots
  and separated withheld Sentinel-2 *worse* (η² 0.085 vs 0.138), so we did not adopt it. The
  disagreement became a measured label uncertainty instead.

## Known limits

- No ground truth exists, so absolute accuracy is untestable; only internal consistency and
  independent optical agreement can be checked.
- A 60-day gap (14 Aug → 13 Oct) brackets the entire maturity and harvest of maize, bajra and
  groundnut — their yield-forming period is sampled once.
- **Crop labels dominate the error budget, not the radar.** The carry-forward puts groundnut at 28%
  of Sokhda; the district figure is 0.35%. Running both moves village production 763 t → 570 t.
  Both scenarios are published.
- 43 plots fall outside every swath footprint and are flagged, not dropped.

## Reproducing

Attach the competition dataset, enable Internet (a 46 MB DEM is fetched at run time), and run the
notebook top to bottom — about 25–40 minutes on CPU. It ends with assertions checking that all 966
plots carry a positive finite forecast, that every village interval brackets its central value, and
that every village mean reproduces its stated anchor. It has been verified to reproduce both output
tables **exactly** from a fresh kernel.
